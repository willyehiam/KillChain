# Global Infrastructure Corpus

## Simulation purpose

Represent the networks and strategic facilities that create national capacity,
military reach, economic resilience, and cascading consequences.

## Domains

1. Electricity generation, storage, transmission regions, and interconnectors.
2. Oil and gas production, refining, storage, terminals, and pipelines.
3. Nuclear fuel cycle and public civilian nuclear facilities.
4. Ports, canals, straits, anchorages, shipyards, and dry docks.
5. Civil and military airfields.
6. Rail, road, bridges, tunnels, mountain passes, and river transport.
7. Undersea cables, public landing regions, and backbone connectivity.
8. Satellites, space launch, tracking, and ground stations.
9. Water, dams, irrigation, and major river systems.
10. Semiconductor, electronics, aircraft, vehicle, ship, steel, chemical, and
    munitions industry.
11. Food, fertilizer, and strategic commodity networks.
12. Government, command, and emergency coordination facilities.

## Representation rule

Use the least granular representation that still creates the intended player
decision.

1. Globally consequential public facilities may be individual entities.
2. Regional networks should be nodes, edges, capacity, redundancy, and demand.
3. Local detail should be aggregated.
4. Unpublished or sensitive internal structure should be synthetic.
5. Vulnerability should emerge from capacity and dependencies, not a list of
   attack instructions.

## Required fields

1. Owner and operator.
2. Function.
3. Capacity and unit.
4. Dependencies.
5. Geographic coverage.
6. Public fixed geometry or modeled region.
7. Commissioning and status dates.
8. Source and confidence.
9. Simulation representation proposal.
10. Maintenance trigger.
