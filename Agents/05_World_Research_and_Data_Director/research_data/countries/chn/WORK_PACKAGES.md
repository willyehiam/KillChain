# China Research Work Packages

## Operating rule

Each package can run independently after the shared identity and source conventions are loaded. Researchers stage sources and atomic claims first. No package may assert completeness from a single summary source.

## Package 1: Politics and institutions

Scope: party, state, Central Military Commission, institutional succession, provincial authority, elite coalitions, legitimacy, and mobilization authority.

Deliverables:

1. Dated government and institutional organization records.
2. Twenty plausible political actors where public evidence supports inclusion.
3. Legal and practical decision authority for war, emergency action, mobilization, treaties, sanctions, and succession.
4. Party, faction, coalition, and institutional relationship records.
5. Election, resignation, death, incapacity, coup, and constitutional succession constraints.
6. Contradiction register for disputed practical authority.

Acceptance: every actor has a dated role, institutional base, eligibility constraints, relationships, and source identifiers. Game outcomes are not inferred from biography alone.

## Package 2: Economy, trade, finance, and resources

Scope: the opening macro economy and the capacities that create player decisions rather than clerical budgeting.

Deliverables:

1. National accounts and sector structure at the bookmark.
2. Trade partners, critical imports, exports, finance channels, reserves, debt, and sanctions exposure.
3. Strategic commodities, food, fuel, industrial inputs, and substitution constraints.
4. Production, consumption, stockpile, import, export, and disruption measures kept separate.
5. Regional economic nodes only where geography changes resilience or access.

Acceptance: quantities declare units, period, nominal or real basis, and source vintage. National totals reconcile with represented regional capacity or carry an exception.

## Package 3: Complete military ledger

Scope: complete national force accounting beyond Justice Mission, with crisis deployments represented only as overlays.

Deliverables:

1. Ministry, joint command, service, component, major command, formation, and unit hierarchy.
2. Complete equipment type catalog across maritime, air, ground, missile defense, space, cyber, information, command, logistics, and support roles.
3. Individual named platforms where identity changes command, mission, loss, or player decision.
4. Quantity conserving pools for all other equipment.
5. Active, committed, transit, training, maintenance, reserve, stored, construction, damaged, destroyed, export, retirement, capture, and unknown states.
6. Home stations, operating regions, readiness uncertainty, crews, munitions, sustainment, and mobilization dependencies.
7. Reconciliation tables that prevent duplicate platforms, formations, and inventories.

Acceptance: every service and relevant government force has a coverage disposition; named platform totals reconcile with class totals; no mission platform exists outside the ledger.

## Package 4: Public fixed facilities and basing

Scope: military and dual use locations whose identity creates a distinct decision about access, capacity, resilience, timing, substitution, or political risk.

Deliverables:

1. Bases, ports, airfields, headquarters, depots, arsenals, training areas, communications sites, and maintenance facilities.
2. Function, controller, supported force, capacity measure, dependencies, and public location precision.
3. Home station relationships without asserting the current location of mobile assets.
4. Alternate facilities and substitution constraints.

Acceptance: exact geometry is used only when a public source supports it. Otherwise use an approximate area, administrative region, or unknown location.

## Package 5: Strategic industry and conversion

Scope: shipbuilding, aerospace, vehicles, missiles, electronics, semiconductors, munitions, repair, and civilian capacity that can materially support mobilization.

Deliverables:

1. Major firms, public plants, yards, research centers, and supply chain clusters.
2. Product families, throughput measures, bottlenecks, skilled labor, imported inputs, and conversion delay.
3. Distinction among announced, ordered, under construction, accepted, and operational output.
4. Regional pools for repetitive capacity and individual nodes for strategic bottlenecks.

Acceptance: peacetime output is not automatically treated as wartime surge capacity. Every conversion assumption is labeled designed until independently reviewed.

## Package 6: Energy, transport, communications, and logistics

Scope: national networks and the nodes that determine military reach, economic endurance, and civilian resilience.

Deliverables:

1. Energy production, imports, refining, storage, generation regions, and major interconnectors.
2. Ports, rail, road, air cargo, inland waterways, pipelines, and maritime routes.
3. Undersea cables, landing regions, terrestrial communications, data centers, satellite ground segments, and redundancy.
4. Military fuel, lift, sealift, tanker, replenishment, maintenance, and distribution dependencies.
5. Network edges, capacity, disruption, repair, substitution, and political access constraints.

Acceptance: networks remain functional graphs rather than decorative facility lists. Sensitive precision is not invented when regional capacity is sufficient for gameplay.

## Package 7: Geography, provinces, and terrain

Scope: provinces, autonomous regions, municipalities, special administrative regions, theater command geography, ports, bases, industrial corridors, and maritime approaches.

Deliverables:

1. Province level administrative geometry and stable identifiers.
2. Terrain, elevation, rivers, coasts, islands, straits, approaches, and chokepoints relevant to movement and sustainment.
3. Population and economic overlays at an abstraction appropriate for global play.
4. Relationships among administrative, military, economic, and infrastructure regions.
5. Map generalization levels for global, theater, and operational zoom.

Acceptance: geographic layers use recognized public datasets, preserve licenses and vintages, and do not imply tactical precision from generalized borders.

## Package 8: Crises, alliances, sanctions, and deployments

Scope: Taiwan coercion, quarantine, blockade, strike, amphibious, counter intervention, escalation, and global economic exposure choices.

Deliverables:

1. Treaty and alliance obligations, consultation rules, access agreements, and practical uncertainty.
2. Active wars, crises, sanctions, exercises, patrols, and foreign commitments at the bookmark.
3. Dated deployment observations linked to the complete force ledger.
4. Political authorization and escalation requirements for intervention.
5. Competing commitments and the risk created by redeployment.

Acceptance: an alliance is not automatic war entry. A crisis roster is a disposition overlay and never a substitute for the complete national inventory.

## Package 9: Bookmark integration

Scope: convert accepted lane records into one explicit opening state at 00:00 UTC on 1 September 2025.

Deliverables:

1. Government, actor, economic, military, infrastructure, alliance, sanctions, crisis, and deployment state references.
2. Historical information cutoff report.
3. Post cutoff knowledge firewall audit.
4. Unresolved assumptions and alternate initialization candidates.
5. Cross lane identity, quantity, and time reconciliation.

Acceptance: every populated field traces to accepted claims; later events are optional pressure paths rather than predetermined knowledge; unresolved evidence remains visible.
