# KillWeb Research Coverage Program

## Purpose

Turn the approved global scope into a finite, reviewable sequence of datasets.

The program avoids two failure modes:

1. Building only the first crisis and leaving the rest of the world inert.
2. Attempting a one to one model of the entire planet before proving what detail
   creates fun decisions.

## Coverage states

Every country, theater, event chain, and infrastructure dataset uses one state:

1. `shell`: required files and identifiers exist.
2. `collecting`: sources and claims are being added.
3. `needs_review`: minimum coverage is present but acceptance gates are not
   complete.
4. `verified`: accepted for the stated historical cutoff and representation
   tier.
5. `stale`: review date passed or a material event changed the record.
6. `deprecated`: replaced but retained for provenance.

## Global force inventory requirement

Every top 80 country uses the same full force accounting standard defined in
`FULL_FORCE_INVENTORY_PROGRAM.md`.

1. Crisis rosters are disposition overlays, not total force lists.
2. Every service, reserve component, and strategically relevant government force
   is included.
3. Every platform and formation belongs to an individual record or quantity
   conserving pool.
4. Readiness, maintenance, training, storage, construction, and current
   commitments remain distinct states.
5. The entire force can respond to the campaign, subject to time, access,
   sustainment, command, and political constraints.
6. Map aggregation changes presentation and control resolution, not inventory.
7. No mission package may use a platform absent from the national force ledger.
8. Losses, transfers, production, repairs, captures, and retirements must
   reconcile against the ledger.

Research order may differ by country. Completeness standards do not.

## Country research lanes

Each top 80 country dossier is divided into eight independent lanes.

1. Politics and institutions.
2. Economy, trade, finance, and resources.
3. Military organization and inventory.
4. Public fixed facilities and basing.
5. Strategic industry and conversion capacity.
6. Energy, transport, communications, and logistics.
7. Geography, provinces, terrain, rivers, and maritime access.
8. Active crises, alliances, sanctions, and foreign deployments.

A country advances to `needs_review` only when all eight lanes have a coverage
assessment, even if some values remain unknown.

## Political actor roster

The target is twenty plausible actors per playable country, not twenty arbitrary
names.

Candidate categories:

1. Current head of state or government.
2. Constitutional successors.
3. Major governing party leaders.
4. Major opposition leaders.
5. Senior military leaders with political relevance.
6. Regional executives with national potential.
7. Coalition brokers.
8. Movement or union leaders.
9. Technocratic or business figures with a documented political role.
10. Exiled or insurgent leadership where relevant.

Every actor needs a dated role, institutional base, ideology tags, relationships,
eligibility constraints, and source record. Coup, election, death, resignation,
and succession systems may select only plausible candidates for that context.

### Aggregation rule

A node is represented individually only when it creates a distinct decision
about access, capacity, resilience, timing, substitution, or political risk.

Repetitive assets are aggregated into regional capacity pools. Players may zoom
into individual facilities only where individual identity matters.

## Mobile asset rule

1. Ships, aircraft, satellites, and mobile ground units receive routes and dated
   observations.
2. Historical coordinates expire after their evidence window.
3. Routine global traffic is sampled and aggregated.
4. Important named formations and platforms may be individual entities.
5. The research corpus does not claim live locations.
6. Scenario initialization converts historical evidence into a dated starting
   state with explicit uncertainty.
