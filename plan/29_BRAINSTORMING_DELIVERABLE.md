# Brainstorming Deliverable

## Direct answer

The current architecture thesis is not the completed answer to the brainstorming
stage.

It answers one important question:

> What general shape should a deterministic, data driven, adaptive modern grand
> strategy simulation take, and what can we learn from open source grand strategy
> content?

The brainstorming stage asks a larger question:

> What is the complete player experience of KillWeb, why is it fun for several
> hours, and which decisions deserve detail at each command resolution?

## What is already strong

1. One persistent world.
2. Real time with pause.
3. The player as the strategic will of a state.
4. National, theater, and mission command resolutions.
5. Progressive disclosure rather than immediate complexity.
6. A shared causal simulation across all resolutions.
7. Genuine faction specific fog of war.
8. Mission packages with real platforms, support, routes, inventory, and
   authorization.
9. Deterministic state transitions and replay.
10. Universal country systems plus archetypes and bespoke content.
11. Structured country AI rather than an LLM controlling truth state.
12. A disciplined multiagent studio model for later production.

These are design pillars. They are not yet the whole game.

## What must still be answered

### 1. The minute to minute national loop

What does the player repeatedly notice, decide, spend, schedule, and observe when
no war is active?

### 2. The peacetime game

How are economy, industry, infrastructure, science, technology, culture,
religion, ideology, elections, legitimacy, diplomacy, intelligence, and force
development fun before crisis begins?

### 3. The crisis loop

How do ambiguous incidents become political decisions, mobilization, signaling,
coercion, alliance consultation, covert action, negotiation, or war?

### 4. The theater loop

How does the player translate national objectives into fronts, access, basing,
logistics, readiness, force posture, campaign plans, and rules of engagement?

### 5. The mission loop

When is opening a Maven style workbench rewarding, when should the game recommend
a plan, and how does a player return to strategic command without losing context?

### 6. Delegation

Which decisions default to ministers and commanders, how does the player express
intent, and what happens when institutions are incompetent, disloyal, overloaded,
or pursuing their own interests?

### 7. Progression

What gets better over a campaign: institutions, doctrine, personnel, industrial
capacity, intelligence coverage, technology, alliance integration, or the
player's own understanding?

### 8. Open ended success

How does the game communicate success, failure, national objectives, negotiated
settlements, regime survival, and historical divergence without forcing a single
victory screen?

### 9. Country differentiation

Which mechanics are universal, which depend on regime and institutions, which are
regional, and which deserve bespoke content?

### 10. Time and attention

What updates every second, hour, day, week, and month? What can pause the game?
What deserves an alert, recommendation, or quiet background resolution?

### 11. Onboarding

What exactly happens in the first ten minutes, the first hour, and the first
campaign session? Which advanced systems are initially delegated?

### 12. Representative campaign

Can the team narrate one complete campaign from bookmark selection through
preparation, crisis, conflict or settlement, and continued alternate history,
including the player's actual repeated inputs?

## Exit artifact

Before brainstorming can be approved, the repository should contain:

1. A short creative constitution.
2. A complete representative campaign walkthrough.
3. A minute to minute loop for national, theater, and mission play.
4. A peacetime loop.
5. A crisis and escalation loop.
6. A war and settlement loop.
7. A delegation model.
8. A progression model.
9. A country differentiation model.
10. An abstraction ledger with reasons.
11. A first ten minute onboarding flow.
12. A list of open questions explicitly accepted for research or later design.

## Approval rule

Documents are provisional until the founder explicitly approves the
brainstorming gate. Starting the research corpus does not imply that approval.
