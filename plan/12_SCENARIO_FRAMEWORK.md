# Scenario Framework

## One world, several bookmarks

KillWeb should use one global simulation with several possible historical
bookmarks. A bookmark changes the starting state, not the fundamental engine.

## Scenario purpose

A scenario provides:

1. A starting date.
2. Verified world state.
3. Country beliefs.
4. Existing plans.
5. Active crises.
6. Opening objectives.
7. Tutorial focus.
8. Suggested playable countries.
9. Historical momentum.
10. Clear divergence labeling.

## Scenario boundaries

A scenario is not:

1. A sealed regional map.
2. A mandatory historical event chain.
3. A fixed victory condition.
4. A substitute for the world simulation.

## Justice Mission lineage

The first prototype used Justice Mission 2025 as a historical reference and
fictionally extended an exercise into July 2026.

The inherited sourcebook records:

1. Five publicly announced exercise zones.
2. Reported aircraft sortie levels.
3. Peak PLA Navy presence.
4. Peak China Coast Guard presence.
5. Identified ships and Coast Guard hulls.
6. Ground Force fires.
7. Rocket Force warning indicators.
8. Civil aviation disruption.
9. Historical and fictional divergence.

## Justice Mission starting premise

A declared exercise approaches its termination time. It does not terminate.
Coast Guard and naval patterns begin to resemble a persistent control regime.
Commercial and civilian access deteriorates while the legal and political status
remains ambiguous.

This creates a tutorial problem:

1. Is it an exercise?
2. Is it a safety regime?
3. Is it a quarantine?
4. Is it a blockade?
5. Is it cover for a wider operation?

## Accuracy rules inherited from the scenario

1. A sortie is not a unique airframe.
2. Peak presence is not a unique platform total.
3. A formation is not a launcher count.
4. A fired rocket is not a launch vehicle.
5. Unknown escorts remain unknown.
6. Reported force levels carry source and confidence.
7. Scenario inventions are labeled.

## Replayability

A scenario remains replayable through:

1. Different country beliefs.
2. Different domestic coalitions.
3. Different AI strategies.
4. Different force readiness.
5. Different intelligence success.
6. Different alliance decisions.
7. Different accidents and miscalculations.
8. Different player actions.

## Campaign continuity

If a player resolves the opening crisis, the campaign continues. The initial
story is a doorway into the world.
