# Vision and Player Fantasy

## Fantasy

The player controls the strategic direction of a contemporary country across
economics, industry, science, technology, diplomacy, politics, culture, religion,
intelligence, military affairs, infrastructure, and territory.

The player can remain at a legible Civilization scale, descend into HOI4 style
operational planning, or open a Maven style workbench for one important
intelligence problem or mission.

## What the player should feel

1. The world is larger than the current war.
2. Every country has its own interests and internal constraints.
3. A distant port, satellite, refinery, election, airbase, shipping route, or
   alliance dispute may change an operation.
4. The player can understand why a plan is possible.
5. The player can understand why a plan failed.
6. The player can delegate routine execution.
7. The player can intervene personally when a decision is interesting.
8. Alternate history feels earned rather than random.

## What the player is not

1. A literal single official restricted to one job description.
2. A god who can create effects without resources.
3. A clerk required to approve every routine movement.
4. An omniscient observer of enemy truth.
5. A commander trapped forever inside one regional scenario.

## Forms of agency

The player acts through:

1. Policy.
2. Budgets.
3. Appointments.
4. Strategic priorities.
5. Laws and authorities.
6. Procurement.
7. Diplomacy.
8. Intelligence priorities.
9. Command intent.
10. Direct intervention in selected operations.

## Grand strategy outside war

KillWeb should remain compelling through:

1. Industrial policy.
2. Energy security.
3. Technology competition.
4. Trade and sanctions.
5. Alliance building.
6. Domestic coalition management.
7. Cultural and ideological influence.
8. Infrastructure investment.
9. Resource access.
10. Intelligence competition.
11. Crisis mediation.
12. Deterrence.

## Alternate history philosophy

The player may pursue morally reckless, politically radical, strategically
unconventional, or historically unlikely strategies when the simulated capacity
and consequences support them.

Plausibility comes from requirements and reactions rather than a menu that simply
forbids divergence.

## Small state fantasy

A smaller country should remain interesting through geography, finance,
technology, diplomacy, resources, intelligence access, culture, institutional
quality, coalition leverage, or control of a chokepoint. Military mass is only
one form of power.
