# Name and Language

## Current name

KillWeb

## Rationale

KillChain accurately described the first prototype because the primary mechanic
was one sequence from collection through assessment.

The expanded game includes:

1. National power.
2. Political institutions.
3. Economy.
4. Industry.
5. Culture and ideology.
6. Alliances.
7. Infrastructure.
8. Logistics.
9. Intelligence.
10. Theater campaigns.
11. Many simultaneous engagement chains.
12. Global reactions and consequences.

The relevant structure is therefore a web rather than a single chain.

## Terminology distinction

### C5ISR network

The people, organizations, communications, computers, intelligence,
surveillance, and reconnaissance infrastructure that supports awareness and
command.

### Decision web

The flow of evidence, interpretation, authority, and coordination that produces
a decision.

### Engagement chain

One operational path from detection and understanding through tasking, effects,
and assessment.

### Effects web

The combined consequences produced across military, political, economic,
informational, diplomatic, and social systems.

### KillWeb

The game and overall simulation connecting all of the above.

## Language principles

1. Prefer plain strategic language over unexplained acronyms.
2. Use military terminology when it improves precision.
3. Explain every specialized term in context.
4. Do not use jargon as atmosphere.
5. Distinguish truth, belief, observation, inference, decision, execution, and
   assessment.
6. Distinguish platform, formation, sortie, package, and effect.
7. Distinguish desired effect from weapon selection.

## Open brand questions

1. Final capitalization and wordmark.
2. Whether KillWeb is the product name or a working title.
3. Whether the name overemphasizes military action relative to economics and
   politics.
4. Whether a subtitle should communicate modern grand strategy.
