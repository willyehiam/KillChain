# Scope and World Model

## World scope

KillWeb should eventually simulate one contemporary world in which every
recognized country can act, trade, align, compete, experience crises, and affect
other countries.

The long term playable target is approximately the 80 highest GDP nations.

## Simulation resolution

The world uses several connected resolutions:

1. Global strategic resolution for national economics, diplomacy, technology,
   politics, and force posture.
2. Regional resolution for trade, infrastructure, bases, logistics, crises, and
   theater forces.
3. Formation resolution for active military theaters.
4. Platform and track resolution for selected missions and scarce strategic
   assets.

These are views of one world state. They are not separate games.

## Geographic levels

Provisional levels include:

1. World.
2. Country.
3. Administrative or strategic region.
4. City, base, port, airfield, or strategic site.
5. Route and network segment.
6. Theater.
7. Formation.
8. Platform or intelligence track.

## Individually represented objects

An object should be individual when its identity, location, readiness, loss, or
availability creates a meaningful decision.

Likely examples:

1. Aircraft carriers.
2. Major surface combatants.
3. Submarines when detected or tasked.
4. Strategic bombers.
5. Scarce tanker and airborne early warning aircraft.
6. Major air defense batteries.
7. Strategic missile formations.
8. Satellites and important constellations.
9. Major ports and airbases.
10. Critical strategic infrastructure.

## Aggregated objects

An object should be aggregated when individual representation adds repetitive
management without changing strategy.

Likely examples:

1. Routine civilian demand.
2. Local distribution infrastructure.
3. Large numbers of similar infantry and support units outside an active
   theater.
4. Common civilian vehicles.
5. Small facilities whose effect is primarily regional capacity.
6. Routine commercial traffic at distant zoom.

## Dynamic aggregation

At distant zoom, formations and regional flows are primary. At closer zoom,
formations can reveal subordinate units and platforms.

Aggregation must preserve:

1. Count.
2. Inventory.
3. Damage.
4. Readiness.
5. Location bounds.
6. Orders.
7. Supply.
8. Identity uncertainty.

## Open world campaign

No scenario should impose a narrow endpoint. A Taiwan crisis may expand into
global economic pressure, alliance restructuring, proxy conflict, operations in
other theaters, domestic upheaval, blockade, negotiation, limited war, or major
war.

## Weather

The founder suggested that weather can initially be abstracted. Distance, basing,
fuel, tankers, airfield survival, reach, access, and logistics are more important
for the first military model.

This remains a provisional abstraction decision.

## Realism boundary

The game should not chase exact representation of every person, vehicle, wire,
substation, warehouse, or local command post. It should represent the network
dependencies and bottlenecks that create strategic decisions.
