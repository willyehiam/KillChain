# Core Gameplay Loops

## Primary global loop

1. Read the world.
2. Interpret incomplete information.
3. Choose objectives.
4. Allocate political and material capacity.
5. Approve, modify, or delegate plans.
6. Observe execution and reactions.
7. Assess consequences.
8. Adapt.

This loop must remain understandable to a new player.

## Military and intelligence loop

1. Discover.
2. Characterize.
3. Maintain custody.
4. Prioritize.
5. Nominate.
6. Select a desired effect.
7. Compose a package.
8. Authorize.
9. Execute.
10. Assess.
11. Restrain, retask, restrike, or escalate.

## Economic loop

1. Identify national vulnerability or opportunity.
2. Choose an economic objective.
3. Secure finance, inputs, labor, technology, and political support.
4. Build or redirect capacity.
5. Connect production to infrastructure and markets.
6. Absorb foreign and domestic reactions.
7. Measure resilience and strategic leverage.
8. Adapt.

## Political loop

1. Form an agenda.
2. Build a governing coalition.
3. Spend credibility, authority, money, or coercive capacity.
4. Issue policy.
5. Manage institutional and factional resistance.
6. Observe public and elite consequences.
7. Preserve, reform, or replace the coalition.

## Diplomatic loop

1. Identify the desired alignment or concession.
2. Read the other actor’s interests and constraints.
3. Build leverage through incentives, guarantees, access, pressure, or
   coalition support.
4. Negotiate or signal.
5. Test compliance.
6. Update trust and credibility.
7. Escalate, compromise, or disengage.

## Science and technology loop

1. Choose strategic capability gaps.
2. Fund research, talent, institutions, and industry.
3. Acquire knowledge domestically or externally.
4. Convert research into production and doctrine.
5. Protect the ecosystem from disruption.
6. Observe diffusion, countermeasures, and obsolescence.

## Information and influence loop

1. Define an audience and desired belief or behavior.
2. Collect audience and network information.
3. Select overt, covert, diplomatic, cultural, or media channels.
4. Commit credibility and resources.
5. Deliver the campaign.
6. Measure reach, persuasion, backlash, attribution, and counteraction.
7. Adapt.

## Progressive complexity

### New player

1. Receives a concise situation summary.
2. Sees two to four meaningful choices.
3. Receives a recommended plan.
4. Approves broad intent.
5. Learns through consequences.

### Intermediate player

1. Adjusts priorities and constraints.
2. Selects commanders and institutions.
3. Compares courses of action.
4. Opens relevant map modes and dependencies.

### Expert player

1. Edits force composition.
2. Tasks sensors.
3. Changes routes and timing.
4. Selects weapons and support.
5. Adjusts engagement rules and abort conditions.
6. Manages individual strategic assets.

## Opening ten minutes

The desired first experience is a guided crisis that teaches:

1. Reading the map.
2. Understanding one ambiguous problem.
3. Selecting one national posture.
4. Maintaining one important intelligence track.
5. Approving one plan.
6. Seeing the world react.

The tutorial should introduce complexity through consequences rather than menus.
