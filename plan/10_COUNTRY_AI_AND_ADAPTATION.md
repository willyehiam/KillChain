# Country AI and Adaptation

## Goal

Every campaign should develop differently because countries perceive, prioritize,
plan, coordinate, learn, and react differently.

## Country AI loop

1. Observe through the country belief state.
2. Update threats, opportunities, and uncertainty.
3. Update national objectives.
4. Let institutions generate candidate actions.
5. Remove illegal, impossible, unaffordable, or politically unavailable actions.
6. Score candidates.
7. Explore a limited number of future consequences for major decisions.
8. Let leadership arbitrate institutional proposals.
9. Commit resources and authority.
10. Monitor execution.
11. Learn from outcomes.
12. Abandon or revise plans when assumptions fail.

## Institutional planners

Possible planners include:

1. Head of government.
2. Foreign ministry.
3. Defense ministry.
4. Military commands.
5. Intelligence agencies.
6. Finance ministry.
7. Central bank.
8. Industry ministry.
9. Energy ministry.
10. Political party or ruling coalition.
11. Regional governments.
12. Major corporations.
13. Security services.
14. Religious or ideological institutions.

Each sees different information and values different outcomes.

## Candidate evaluation

Actions may be scored against:

1. Strategic objective value.
2. Time.
3. Fiscal cost.
4. Industrial cost.
5. Military risk.
6. Political risk.
7. Public support.
8. Elite support.
9. Alliance effects.
10. Escalation.
11. Uncertainty.
12. Reversibility.
13. Opportunity cost.
14. Institutional preference.
15. Leader personality.

## Strategic memory

Countries should remember:

1. Agreements kept or broken.
2. Threats that succeeded or failed.
3. Player force posture.
4. Repeated routes and tactics.
5. Recent losses.
6. Intelligence failures.
7. Alliance support.
8. Domestic backlash.
9. Economic coercion.
10. Past concessions.

Memory should decay and may be politically interpreted.

## Plan structure

A plan should include:

1. Objective.
2. Preconditions.
3. Assumptions.
4. Phases.
5. Required capacity.
6. Indicators of success.
7. Abort conditions.
8. Alternative branches.
9. Review interval.
10. Responsible institutions.

Historical plans provide starting momentum, not predetermined outcomes.

## Adaptation

AI can adapt through:

1. Reweighting strategies.
2. Updating beliefs about the player.
3. Countering observed tactics.
4. Diversifying routes and force posture.
5. Reallocating collection.
6. Changing alliance strategy.
7. Abandoning invalid plans.
8. Learning which institutions are reliable.
9. Increasing or reducing risk tolerance.
10. Choosing among alternate national futures.

4. Maritime trading state.
5. Continental land power.
6. Energy exporter.
7. Technology power.

14. Alliance hub.
15. Nonaligned broker.

Countries may have several archetypes and can change over time.

## Strategic personalities

Play and AI personalities may include:

1. Aggressive.
2. Defensive.
3. Deceptive.
4. Economic.
5. Logistics focused.
6. Information focused.
7. Coalition focused.
8. Risk averse.
9. Opportunistic.
10. Ideological.

## Difficulty philosophy

Higher difficulty should improve:

1. Planning horizon.
2. Candidate quality.
3. Coordination.
4. Information processing.
5. Adaptation.
6. Resource allocation.
7. Detection of player patterns.
8. Plan review.

It should not rely primarily on invisible production or combat bonuses.

## LLM boundary

Language models may assist with:

1. Advisor dialogue.
2. Situation summaries.
3. Decision explanation.
4. Offline content generation.
5. Research synthesis.
6. Playtest personalities.

They should not directly mutate authoritative game state or resolve every
simulation tick.
