# Stage Gates

## Current stage: Brainstorming

### Purpose

Define what KillWeb is, who the player is, why it is fun, what makes it distinct,
what it simulates, what it abstracts, and what quality means.

### Required outputs

1. Approved game constitution.
2. Approved player fantasy.
3. Approved core loop.
4. Approved command layers.
5. Approved scope.
6. Approved abstraction principles.
7. Approved historical divergence philosophy.
8. Approved list of major systems.
9. Approved research questions.
10. Explicitly prioritized open questions.

### Completion standard

The brainstorming stage is not complete because an architecture thesis exists.
It is complete only when the team can describe an entire representative campaign
from opening bookmark through peacetime preparation, crisis, conflict, settlement,
and continued play without hiding the fun behind words such as "simulate" or
"abstract."

The canonical completion checklist lives in
`29_BRAINSTORMING_DELIVERABLE.md`.

### Authorized parallel research workstream

The founder has explicitly authorized the World Research and Data Director to
build the research corpus while brainstorming continues.

This exception permits:

1. Research methodology and source policy.
2. Schemas and data dictionaries.
3. Country, theater, infrastructure, and event research queues.
4. Bounded collection of public, date stamped, sourced facts.
5. Geospatial normalization with explicit precision and uncertainty.
6. Contradiction tracking and source review.

This exception does not mean the project has entered or completed the full
research stage. It does not authorize simulation implementation or let collected
data silently decide unresolved game design.

### Prohibited work

1. Building the new simulation engine.
2. Claiming a complete or authoritative world dataset before the research gate.
3. Launching a large coding swarm.
4. Locking a visual system.
5. Choosing a final technical stack.
6. Treating provisional architecture documents as implementation tickets.

## Next stage: Research

### Purpose

Construct a sourced and playable understanding of the contemporary world.

### Intended topics

1. Country institutions and military command structures.
2. Force inventories and publicly documented basing patterns.
3. Alliance obligations and access.
4. Industrial and munitions capacity.
5. Trade, energy, transportation, communications, ports, airfields, pipelines,
   rail, shipping, and undersea cables.
6. Publicly documented strategic infrastructure.
7. Political factions and domestic constraints.
8. Military doctrine, readiness, and mobilization.
9. Current wars, disputes, sanctions, and unresolved claims.
10. Historical starting background for the selected bookmark.

### Gate to leave research

The data must be sufficient to construct a playable world model with confidence,
date, source, and abstraction labels. It does not need to pretend to know every
real asset.
