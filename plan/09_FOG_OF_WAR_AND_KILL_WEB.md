# Fog of War and the KillWeb

## Definition

KillWeb is the network connecting:

1. Sensors.
2. Data transport.
3. Processing.
4. Models.
5. Analysts.
6. Commanders.
7. Authorities.
8. Platforms.
9. Weapons and other effectors.
10. Logistics.
11. Assessment.
12. Strategic consequences.

An engagement chain is one path through that larger web. The game is named for
the full system rather than one strike sequence.

## Truth and belief

The authoritative simulation contains truth. Every country and institution
contains its own belief state.

A belief may be:

1. Accurate.
2. Incomplete.
3. Stale.
4. Contradictory.
5. Duplicated.
6. Deceived.
7. Politically manipulated.
8. Correctly uncertain.
9. Confidently wrong.

AI countries and players act on their available belief state.

## Track lifecycle

Provisional states:

1. Indication.
2. Detection.
3. Correlation.
4. Classification.
5. Identification.
6. Custody.
7. Targetability.
8. Nomination.
9. Authorization.
10. Engagement.
11. Assessment.
12. Closure, loss, split, or regression.

Progress is not permanent. A target can become stale, disappear, split into
several hypotheses, enter a protected environment, deploy deception, or cease
being lawful or reachable.

## Maven inspired decision advantage mechanics

### Data ingestion

Sensors create a high volume stream. Bandwidth, storage, analyst attention, and
processing capacity limit what becomes useful intelligence.

### Probabilistic inference

Models produce confidence and alternative hypotheses, not binary truth.

### Target discovery

Models and analysts search large areas and data volumes for small relevant
signals.

### Positive identification

High consequence actions require evidence thresholds, corroboration, policy, and
human confirmation.

### Signal to noise

The player allocates processing, model, and collection capacity to priorities.
Adversaries can increase clutter and deception.

### Baseline anomaly detection

Normal patterns must be learned or defined before deviations become meaningful.

### Model accreditation

Models have domain, training, performance, and accreditation limits. A high
performing maritime model may be unreliable for ground imagery or a new theater.

### Responsible use

Low accreditation, poor data, domain shift, and operator pressure increase false
positive risk.

### Human authorization

AI may recommend. It does not automatically authorize lethal force.

### Sensor constellation management

Satellites and other scarce sensors have coverage, revisit, priority, collection
geometry, weather or environment limits, and competing requests.

### Cross command handoff

Intelligence can lose freshness, context, confidence, or authority as it moves
between organizations.

### Combat assessment

Assessment is a new intelligence problem. It does not automatically reveal
ground truth.

## Mission package principle

The player starts with a desired effect, not a weapon icon.

Possible effects include:

1. Delay.
2. Deter.

4. Degrade.
5. Disrupt.
6. Destroy.
7. Isolate.
8. Protect.
9. Deceive.

## Package causality

The package must show:

1. What is delivering the effect.
2. Where it is located.
3. Whether it is visible to the player.
4. Its reach.
5. Its path.
6. Its readiness.
7. Its available inventory.
8. Its support dependencies.
9. Conflicts with other missions.
10. Expected return or loss.

## Assessment rules

A high probability assessment is not identical to truth.

The simulation maintains the actual damage state. The player sees evidence and
confidence.

If a target is assessed as destroyed but continues moving, the game must explain
one of the following:

1. The assessment was wrong.
2. The track represents a decoy or different entity.
3. The track correlation was wrong.
4. The observed movement is recovery, towing, or residual activity.
5. The implementation is broken.

The interface may never quietly display contradictory states.
