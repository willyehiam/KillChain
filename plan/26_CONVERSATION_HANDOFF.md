# Conversation Handoff

## Purpose

This is a chronological, content faithful reconstruction of the project
conversation for another AI or collaborator.

It preserves durable founder intent, requirements, critiques, decisions, and
design evolution. It is not a byte exact transcript. Uploaded screenshots and
the original PDF remain external references unless separately added to the
repository.

## 01: Original vision

The founder asked for a highly performant web based war simulator using WebGPU or
WebGL.

The initial theater list included:

1. Taiwan Strait.
2. Ukraine and Russia.
3. Irregular conflict in Europe.
4. Iran, the United States, Israel, and Saudi Arabia.
5. Egypt, Ethiopia, Sudan, and surrounding actors.
6. Kashmir, Pakistan, India, and China.
7. North and South Korea.
8. The United States and Cuba.
9. China, the United States, the Pacific, and the American west coast.
10. The Bering Strait.
11. The Malacca Strait.
12. The Strait of Hormuz.
13. The South China Sea.

The founder wanted information warfare, cyber operations, gray zone activity,
covert action, political influence, economic pressure, maritime pressure,
infrastructure disruption, and alternative history.

Civilian ferries, merchant shipping, containerized capabilities, data centers,
energy systems, supply lines, military bases, undersea cables, pipelines,
political violence, election influence, and separatist support were discussed as
examples of the breadth of modern competition.

The desired game references were Civilization 6, Civilization 7, Hearts of Iron
III, Hearts of Iron IV, Palantir Gotham, Maven, Anduril, technical mono design,
and surveillance interface aesthetics.

## 02: Kill chain clarification

The founder clarified that the game must let the player execute a real kill
chain:

1. Gather intelligence.
2. Reveal possible targets.
3. Find the actual target.
4. Maintain custody while the target deploys countermeasures.
5. Choose a package.
6. Execute.
7. Assess.

This could not remain a vague mission button.

## 03: Depth and quality demand

The founder repeatedly warned against making the game too simple.

Future playtest and critique roles requested included:

1. United States, China, Taiwan, and secondary actors under fog of war.
2. Aggressive, defensive, deceptive, economic, logistics, and information
   personalities.
3. Exploit hunters.
4. Kill chain auditors.
5. Systems auditors.
6. Performance agents.
7. Interface agents.
8. Cross browser visual auditors.
9. An Armchair General critic hostile to shallow simulation.

The founder requested continuous build, test, critique, and improvement loops.

## 04: First playable version

The first build produced a Taiwan Strait command screen with tracks, decision
stages, package composition, execution, and assessment.

The initial interface used a dark technical aesthetic and a line drawn map.

## 05: First major critique

The founder reported:

1. The joint effects workbench did not work.
2. The interface looked poor.
3. The map was not visibly a real map.
4. The design felt performative rather than intuitive.
5. The mechanics were not sufficiently game like.

The founder emphasized that Maven exists to make complex data understandable to
ordinary operators. KillWeb therefore needs clarity, not dense technical theater.

4. Probabilistic confidence.
5. Human authorization.
6. Needle in the haystack search.
7. Positive identification.

15. Cross command handoff.

17. Signal to noise management.

Many Maven and Palantir screenshots were supplied showing maps, imagery, target

## 07: Map first decision

The founder decided:

1. The map is the base screen.
2. Everything overlays the map.
3. Use actual geographic borders.
4. Support pan and zoom.
5. Support dark vector and satellite views.
6. Use designed theater and interest overlays.
7. Use clear military and civilian symbols.
8. Make mobile units visibly move.
9. Make time acceleration feel alive.
10. Show a globe and satellites at distant zoom.

The desired visual balance became approximately half Brass Hands and half
Palantir.

## 08: Core loop and progressive complexity

A dedicated game design role was requested.

The founder decided that the player should not be thrown into every mechanic.
The basic loop must be simple, understandable, and repeatable. Advanced depth is
revealed progressively.

## 09: Founder answers on game scope

The founder clarified:

1. The player controls a broad scope similar to Civilization and HOI4.
2. Fun matters more than perfectly simulating one government job.
3. Microscopic detail should be abstracted when it is not fun.
4. Victory may be self defined and open ended.
5. A regional scenario can expand into global strategy.
6. Reckless but plausible strategies should remain possible.
7. The game uses one world.
8. Campaigns may last several hours or persist across sessions.
9. Time is real time with pause.
10. The Indo Pacific involves many allied and regional actors.
11. Granularity may vary among nation, city, base, formation, and platform.
12. The default map is dark vector geography with satellite toggle.
13. Formations aggregate at distant zoom and reveal units at closer zoom.
14. The game uses high level intent and optional mission detail.
15. Domains include maritime, air, ground, space, cyber, information, logistics,
    political, and economic activity.
16. Routes, range, fuel, tankers, airfields, readiness, and basing matter.
17. Contemporary capabilities should be represented as realistically as the
    research supports.
18. The opening ten minutes should serve as a tutorial.
19. Visual direction is half Brass Hands and half Palantir.
20. The game must work reliably.

## 10: Adaptive AI

The founder asked whether campaigns can remain replayable because AI adapts.

The resulting direction was:

1. Use shared country systems.
2. Use country strategies and archetypes.
3. Use utility scoring.
4. Add genuine belief state.
5. Add memory.
6. Add opponent modeling.
7. Add plan invalidation.
8. Avoid relying on LLM calls for every simulation decision.

## 11: Justice Mission research

The founder supplied five exact public exercise polygons and a PDF about Justice
Mission 2025.

The supplied analysis distinguished:

1. 207 aircraft sorties from unique airframes.
2. 17 PLA Navy ships at peak from unique ship totals.
3. 15 Coast Guard vessels at peak.
4. Two identified Ground Force firing formations.
5. 27 rockets from launcher count.
6. Rocket Force organizations from exact platform quantity.

Named ships, Coast Guard hulls, firing areas, civil aviation effects, and
political reactions were discussed.

The scenario sourcebook in the repository preserves that distinction.

## 12: Effects package causality failure

The founder reported that an effects package named Deep Lantern did not reveal:

1. Whether it used a missile, aircraft, ship, submarine, or land system.
2. What delivered the package.
3. Where the friendly assets were.
4. How the mission reached the target.

The founder also reported that Orchid 31 continued moving after several high
confidence destruction assessments.

This established a nonnegotiable rule:

Every effect needs actual executing and supporting assets, and the truth state,
belief state, movement state, and assessment must remain causally coherent.

## 13: Repository preservation

The founder requested an `OGKillChain` branch to preserve it.

`f6c6ac1a28ef54860ecaab43d0a9c1f332c51a8a`.

## 14: Strategic reset

The founder stepped back from immediate iteration and began asking how to build a
web based game with HOI4 level complexity.

The ambition expanded toward:

1. The ultimate modern geopolitics alternative history game.
2. Any major world leader or country.
3. Civilization style legibility.
4. HOI4 style depth.
5. Maven style optional mission control.
6. A recognizable real world.
7. Approximately 80 playable countries.
8. A fall 2025 bookmark that precedes Justice Mission 2025 and the later
   Venezuela and Iran escalations.

## 15: Stage sequence

The founder established:

1. Brainstorming now.
2. Research next.
3. Game and simulation design later.
4. Experience design later.
5. Engineering and agent orchestration later.
6. Development only after those stages.

The founder explicitly prohibited advancing beyond brainstorming at this point.

## 16: Reference repository study

Two repositories were supplied:

1. Difficulty and AI.
2. Hearts of Iron IV.

The study found:

1. Difficulty and AI is an Europa Universalis IV mod that demonstrates candidate
   generation, state gates, utility evaluation, and scripted economic or war
   behavior.
2. The Hearts of Iron repository is an installed data snapshot rather than the
   engine source.
3. HOI content shows shared country schemas, compact histories, generic focus
   trees, authored strategy plans, event hooks, and tunable constants.
4. The transferable architecture is one universal model plus archetypes,
   regional systems, country data, and bespoke major power content.

## 17: Agent studio concept

The recommended development organization became:

1. One authoritative architecture.
2. Ten specialist roles.
3. At most two or three concurrent production code writers.
4. Isolated worktrees.
5. Structured task packets.
6. Invariant and replay tests.
7. Adversarial review.
8. One integration gate.

## 18: Rename to KillWeb

The founder decided that KillChain had become too narrow.

KillWeb better describes:

1. The network of sensing and belief.
2. National power.
3. Institutions.
4. Logistics.
5. Economic and political systems.
6. Theater campaigns.
7. Mission packages.
8. Cross domain effects.
9. Global consequences.

## 19: Current repository direction

1. Preserve `OGKillChain`.
2. Put all KillWeb work on `main`.
3. Create ten agent folders.
4. Create a comprehensive `plan/` corpus.
5. Create a high level `SimulationEngine/` corpus.
6. Preserve enough context that another AI can continue without repeating the
   original mistakes.
7. Remain in brainstorming.

## 20: Research workstream exception

The founder authorized the research agent to begin building a world data corpus
while the project remains in brainstorming.

The requested research scope includes:

1. Approximately the top 80 countries by GDP.
2. Military institutions, structures, inventories, installations, and public
   basing information.
3. Major military and civilian infrastructure.
4. Energy production, transmission, pipelines, cables, and bottlenecks.
5. Shipyards and dual use industrial capacity.
6. Space launch and satellite infrastructure.
7. Military headquarters and public command sites.
8. Rivers, terrain, and topography.
9. Detailed theater timelines, forces, movements, and political decisions.
10. Political event chains that can alter the starting world.

The founder cited the Justice Mission 2025 reconstruction as the standard for
distinguishing exercise areas, participating organizations, detected forces,
sorties, peak presence, unique platforms, and reported movement.

## 21: Brainstorming is still open

The founder asked whether the architecture study was the answer to the
brainstorming stage.

The answer is no. It is the current architecture thesis. Brainstorming remains
open until the project has a coherent playable campaign model across national,
theater, and mission decisions, including peacetime and nonmilitary play.

## 22: Frequent publishing

The founder instructed the team to push into the repository as often as possible.
Coherent validated planning and research checkpoints should therefore be
published independently to GitHub `main`.
