# Command Layers and Delegation

## Layer 01: National strategy

The player directs:

1. Economy and fiscal policy.
2. Industrial capacity.
3. Trade and resources.
4. Technology and science.
5. Energy.
6. Population and legitimacy.
7. Culture, religion, identity, and ideology.
8. Diplomacy and alliances.
9. Intelligence services.
10. Military procurement.
11. Infrastructure.
12. National force posture.

The interface should summarize structural strengths, weaknesses, dependencies,
and trends.

## Layer 02: Theater command

The player directs:

1. Campaign objectives.
2. Force posture.
3. Basing.
4. Readiness.
5. Logistics.
6. Ammunition allocation.
7. Reinforcement routes.
8. Command relationships.
9. Coalition participation.
10. Rules of engagement.
11. Escalation boundaries.
12. Priority intelligence requirements.

Commanders translate intent into plans.

## Layer 03: Mission command

The player can inspect and edit:

1. Tracks.
2. Sources.
3. Confidence.
4. Freshness.
5. Target identity.
6. Desired effect.
7. Executing platforms.
8. Supporting platforms.
9. Weapons or other capabilities.
10. Routes.
11. Timing.
12. Tankers and refueling.
13. Electronic warfare.
14. Cyber support.
15. Decoys.
16. Collateral constraints.
17. Authorization.
18. Abort conditions.
19. Assessment plans.

## Delegation model

Every layer supports:

1. Intent only.
2. Recommended plan approval.
3. Constraint editing.
4. Full manual planning for selected decisions.
5. Standing doctrine and automatic execution for routine cases.

## Staff recommendations

Staff should provide a small number of genuinely different courses of action.
Each course should state:

1. Intended effect.
2. Required capacity.
3. Time.
4. Risk.
5. Political implications.
6. Assumptions.
7. Dependencies.
8. Likely adversary reactions.
9. Conditions that would invalidate the plan.

## Attention

Direct intervention should not be punished merely because the player enjoys
detail. The game may still model organizational overload, delayed decisions, or
weak coordination at higher difficulty.

Any command attention mechanic should remain light and should not deny the core
fantasy.

## Continuity across layers

Example:

1. National view shows a long range weapon inventory and industrial replacement
   rate.
2. Theater view shows the allocation of those weapons to commands and bases.
3. Mission view shows exactly which weapons a package reserves.
4. Execution removes them from inventory.
5. Assessment changes future demand and doctrine.

No layer may maintain a duplicate fictional inventory.
