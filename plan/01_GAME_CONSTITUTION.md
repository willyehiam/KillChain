# KillWeb Game Constitution

These principles are the provisional constitution for brainstorming. They should
be debated and then approved before the project advances.

## Principle 01: One living world

KillWeb is one persistent global simulation. Regional crises are openings into
the world, not sealed scenarios that erase the rest of geopolitics.

## Principle 02: Recognizable history, systemic divergence

The starting world is historically recognizable. History diverges because actors
make different choices and systems produce different consequences. Major events
are not forced merely because they occurred historically.

## Principle 03: The player is the strategic will of a state

The player has broad control similar to Civilization and HOI4. Institutions,
commanders, factions, allies, and markets create friction and imperfect
execution, but they should deepen strategy rather than convert play into office
work.

## Principle 04: One simulation at several resolutions

National strategy, theater command, and mission command are views of the same
inventories, organizations, infrastructure, beliefs, plans, and events.

## Principle 05: Complexity is discovered

The basic loop must be understandable quickly. Staff recommendations, delegation,
defaults, and progressive disclosure reveal advanced mechanics only when the
player chooses or consequences demand them.

## Principle 06: Every effect has a cause

No strike, cyber effect, blockade, influence campaign, industrial output, or
political outcome appears by magic. The game records the assets, resources,
authority, route, timing, assumptions, execution, and evidence behind it.

## Principle 07: Information is a strategic resource

Every country acts on a belief state rather than omniscient truth. Confidence,
freshness, provenance, deception, contradiction, classification, and access all
matter.

## Principle 08: Modern power is multidomain

Military power interacts with economic, political, technological, cultural,
religious, informational, cyber, space, diplomatic, industrial, and logistical
power.

## Principle 09: Geography creates strategy

Distance, access, bases, routes, ports, airfields, chokepoints, terrain,
infrastructure, and network dependencies shape what countries can do.

## Principle 10: Fun governs fidelity

The game should be as realistic as necessary to create meaningful choices, but it
should abstract details that create repetitive administration rather than
strategy.

## Principle 11: Countries share a deep common model

Supporting many playable countries requires universal systems, strategic
archetypes, regional mechanics, and selective bespoke content. Countries are not
separate handcrafted engines.

## Principle 12: The game remains open

Campaigns do not end at a single canonical victory screen. Players may define
their own ambitions, and the world continues responding.

## Principle 13: Consequences cross systems

Military success can damage alliances, markets, legitimacy, industrial capacity,
or escalation control. Economic success can create military and diplomatic
options. No major system exists in isolation.

## Principle 14: The interface explains rather than performs

Every visual element must help the player perceive, compare, decide, act, or
learn. Technical atmosphere cannot justify tiny text, weak contrast, fake data,
or permanent decorative chrome.

## Principle 15: The simulation must be auditable

Deterministic replay, invariants, decision traces, state provenance, and
assessment evidence are product features, not only engineering concerns.
