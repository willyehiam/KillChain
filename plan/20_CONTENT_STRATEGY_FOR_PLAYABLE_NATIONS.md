# Content Strategy for Playable Nations

## Long term target

Approximately the 80 highest GDP nations should eventually be fully playable.

Every other country should still exist and participate in the world.

## Common model

All countries use common systems for:

1. Government.
2. Economy.
3. Industry.
4. Technology.
5. Population.
6. Politics.
7. Trade.
8. Diplomacy.
9. Military.
10. Intelligence.
11. Infrastructure.
12. Energy.
13. Culture and identity.

## Content tiers

### Tier 01

Approximately 8 to 12 deeply authored major powers and crisis states.

They receive:

1. Detailed institutions.
2. Bespoke strategic plans.
3. Unique internal political structures.
4. Detailed forces.
5. National technology and industrial challenges.
6. Distinct interfaces or workflows only when mechanically justified.

### Tier 02

Approximately 20 to 30 countries with distinct institutions, strategic
positions, regional objectives, military structures, and political constraints.

### Tier 03

Approximately 40 additional countries using universal systems, country data,
regional events, strategic archetypes, and selected unique mechanics.

### Background world

All remaining countries:

1. Trade.
2. Vote.
3. Align.
4. Experience crises.
5. Control territory.
6. Host infrastructure.
7. Affect markets.
8. May later become playable.

## Country identity

A country should feel distinct because of:

1. Geography.
2. Institutions.
3. Population.
4. Economy.
5. Industry.
6. Energy.
7. Technology.
8. Military organization.
9. Alliances.
10. History.
11. Domestic factions.
12. Strategic beliefs.
13. Leader and elite incentives.
14. Available information.

Identity should not rely only on modifier bonuses.

## Archetypes

Country archetypes provide reusable strategic behavior and content without
erasing national specificity.

Examples:

1. Global expeditionary power.
2. Continental military power.
3. Maritime trading power.
4. Nuclear deterrent state.
5. Energy exporter.
6. Technology and finance hub.
7. Fragile conflict state.
8. Alliance dependent state.
9. Chokepoint state.
10. Nonaligned broker.

## Regional systems

Regional mechanics may cover:

1. European institutions.
2. NATO.
3. ASEAN.
4. Gulf security.
5. African regional organizations.
6. South Asian deterrence.
7. Korean security.
8. Arctic competition.
9. Pacific island access.
10. Global shipping chokepoints.

## Bespoke content rule

Bespoke content is justified when:

1. The institution is structurally unique.
2. The country creates an important global decision.
3. Generic systems cannot express the behavior.
4. The mechanic remains reusable enough to maintain.

## Historical paths

Countries begin with several plausible plans rather than one focus tree that
guarantees history.

Plans should:

1. Have preconditions.
2. Consume capacity.
3. React to the world.
4. Contain branches.
5. Be abandoned when assumptions fail.
6. Permit player created alternatives.
